Research date: 2026-11-15. Scope: AI automation / AI agents / Claude Code educators and agency operators who compete with Nate Herk for the same audience.

**How to read the numbers.** Subscriber counts come from third-party trackers (CreatorDB, DeveloperEducators, SocialCounts, iQfluence, SponsorRadar) or from search snippets of YouTube pages. ## (c) Audience pain points with evidence

1. **Demos don't survive production (messy data, edge cases, no tests).**
   - "Most Tutorials Are Nothing But Demos". Tutorials feed clean data, while clients bring messy spreadsheets with duplicates. https://www.browseract.com/blog/why-n8n-workflows-fail-to-make-money-how-to-fix-it
   - n8n has no built-in testing. https://www.pagelines.com/blog/n8n-engineering-limitations
   - "In a demo, one good output is enough but in production, the 5th edge case is what breaks trust." https://techstackbytes.substack.com/p/the-last-mile-problem-in-ai/comments
   - A Skool post: "Most AI systems break the day a real customer touches them." (via search snippet)
   - Many polished n8n screenshots have "never ever been used in a real business" (opinion). https://sentient-chatgptricks.beehiiv.com/p/automation-reality-vs-social-media
2. **"Selling shovels": educators earn from courses, not client results.**
   - Critics say most gurus make money "selling courses / coaching on how to sell AI automation services". https://www.stork.ai/blog/ai-agencies-are-dead-this-is-next
   - A 2026 Reddit roundup: "the model is not a scam, but many people teaching it are running a course grift." https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-automation-agency-scam-reddit
   - Small businesses "do not pay that much money" compared with $300K claims. https://videohighlight.com/v/3YLyuA3oMu0
   - Nate's own exit from his agency in Dec 2025 makes him more exposed to this critique.
3. **No proof or case studies, so beginners can't close clients.** Beginners "can't close pitches because they have no social proof like case studies" (Collab365 persona doc, low confidence). Nate's own AAS community has a thread called "Struggling to sell your first project with n8n?" https://www.skool.com/ai-automation-society/struggling-to-sell-your-first-project-with-n8n
4. **Security is ignored in tutorials while real CVEs hit the tools people teach.**
   - n8n CVE-2026-25049: authenticated users can get RCE, then "decrypt all stored credentials". A patch bypass followed "within 24 hours". "Ni8mare" CVE-2026-21858. Sources: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/critical-n8n-flaws-disclosed-along-with-public-exploits/amp/, https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/n8n-flaw-puts-hundreds-of-thousands-of-enterprise-ai-systems-at-risk/
   - Indirect prompt injection through the n8n AI Agent node and webhooks. https://www.valencesecurity.com/saas-security-terms/n8n-security
   - Only IndyDevDan covers agent security seriously, and he speaks to engineers, not to SMB service sellers.
5. **Multi-agent hype vs reality.**
   - Practitioners report: "6-agent pipeline is slower and less reliable than my old 2-agent one". https://infinisynapse.com/en/blog/multi-agent-workflows-reddit
   - Consensus: "start with one agent and add agents only when measured results require it".
   - A dev.to post found that "1 coder, 1 reviewer and 1 gate worked better" than a swarm. https://dev.to/lars_winstand/i-thought-my-ai-coding-agent-setup-needed-a-swarm-but-1-coder-1-reviewer-and-1-gate-worked-better-15lp
   - **What this means:** the audience wants *evidence for when* org-chart agents pay off. They are skeptical of 30-agent showcases.
6. **Price and value fatigue with Skool, and too many communities.**
   - "every YouTuber that talks about n8n has a Skool community" (r/n8n).
   - Price anchors range from $9 (Claude Code Club) to $129–184/mo (AAS+, Maker School) to $5–7K (AAA).
   - Refund opacity complaints (AAA, Maker School).
7. **Non-English markets are underserved by the big names.** None of the top 8 teaches in another language. Spanish communities such as Imperio Digital ($67→$77/mo) and TribuClaw are monetizing on their own. https://aifunnelinsider.com/imperio-digital-review-2026/
8. **Video and creative automation is emerging but shallow.**
   - Claude Code + Remotion tutorials are plentiful (RoboNuggets, Apidog, blog guides).
   - No **ad-specific** tutorial turned up in this research (search across about 18 results, Nov 2026). The coverage is "make an animation", not "ship performance ad variants for a client, at volume, with brand QA".

## (d) Top positioning opportunities for the user (ranked)

The user's assets: a 32-agent Claude Code plugin modeled as an org chart (branch → planner/executor/reviewer, plus a dedicated security/QA reviewer branch); a JSON-driven motion design toolkit (headless Chromium + ffmpeg, Blender phone mockups); websites and dashboards for LTGPEAK and local SMBs; an Obsidian knowledge system.

### 1. "Agents that pass review": production QA and security for AI builds (highest leverage)
- **Why:** pain points 1, 4 and 5 are the loudest, and the business-side educators (Nate, Nick, Liam, Jono) cover them least. Engineers (Cole, Indy) cover them, but not for people selling to SMBs. The user's **dedicated security/QA reviewer branch** is a ready-made answer that they can show working.
- **Angle:** "Every build my agents ship gets reviewed by a security agent and a QA agent. Here's what they caught." Publish real catch logs: leaked keys, prompt-injection paths, unhandled edge cases.
- **First step:** run the reviewer branch against 3 popular public n8n or Claude Code templates (with permission or public repos). Publish a "Security & QA teardown #1" video and blog post with a reusable checklist. Cite CVE-2026-25049 and the indirect prompt injection risk as context.

### 2. Org-chart multi-agent design, with measurement instead of hype
- **Why:** the audience distrusts "30-agent" showcases (pain point 5). Cole ("software factory") and Indy ("agent teams") are the only serious voices, and both speak to developers. Nobody teaches **org design** (roles, escalation, review gates) to operators.
- **Angle:** "When does adding an agent pay off?" Run benchmarks of single agent vs planner/executor vs planner/executor/reviewer on real tasks: defect rate, cost, time. Pitch it as management theory applied to agents.
- **First step:** pick 1 real task (for example, build a local-business landing page). Run it 3 ways with the plugin and record tokens, wall time and reviewer-found defects. Publish the table and the org chart diagram. This becomes the flagship credibility piece.

### 3. Code-driven video ad factory for brands and local businesses
- **Why:** pain point 8. Remotion + Claude Code content is "make a cool animation". Nobody teaches **performance ad production** from a JSON spec, with a brand-QA reviewer, many variants and phone mockups. The user's toolkit (headless Chromium + ffmpeg + Blender mockups) goes beyond what RoboNuggets and the blog tutorials show. Visual output also makes naturally strong Shorts and Reels.
- **First step:** produce 10 LTGPEAK ad variants from one JSON brief and post the before/after spec next to the outputs. Then offer a fixed-price "10 ads in 48 hours" package to 3 local businesses as paid pilots. Each pilot becomes a case study (which also addresses opportunity 4).

### 4. Real client receipts over demos: the local-business build log
- **Why:** pain points 2 and 3. Nate exited his agency, Liam's critics say "course grift", and Nick's clients are B2B. A small operator who **shows real local clients** (site + dashboard + automation + ads), with numbers, is the credibility play the market says it wants.
- **First step:** pick 1 current local client and publish a public "build log". Cover scope, price, what the agents built, what the reviewer caught, and the result after 30 days. With permission, include the live dashboard. Repeat monthly.

### 5. A productized starter kit instead of yet another $100+/mo Skool
- **Why:** pain point 6. The market is saturated with Skool communities, and price anchors are polarized ($9 vs $129–$184). A **one-time-price plugin or template** sells on proof, not on community FOMO. IndyDevDan's $299 course and the Claude Skills sellers (Samin Yasar) show that product sales work.
- **First step:** package a cut-down "3-branch" version of the org-chart plugin (planner/executor/security reviewer) as a free GitHub repo to build an audience. Keep the full 32-agent version and the video toolkit as a paid tier or a done-for-you service. Track stars and downloads as the demand signal before building a community.
