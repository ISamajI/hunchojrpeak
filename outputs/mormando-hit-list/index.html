01 · Who he is

The story is the asset

He was born and raised in Bensonhurst, Brooklyn, and started acting in his early 40s. He's 56 now. On the Burn Down podcast he describes getting into trouble and going to jail before he found acting. That is a public, on-the-record comeback story, and it is the strongest hook he has.

He plays Gaetano, a series lead on Gravesend (Prime Video). Season 3, Part 1 dropped Sept 25, 2026, with William DeMeo and Shaq attached. That gives him an audience he can borrow right now.

Credits worth leaning on

  • TV: For Life (ABC), Lilyhammer, The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel, FBI: Most Wanted, The Equalizer (2025)
  • Stunt work: Babygirl, No Hard Feelings, The Report
  • Has worked with 50 Cent, Chazz Palminteri, Andrew Dice Clay, Armand Assante, and Steven Van Zandt (per the Burn Down episode notes)
  • Next: The Landfill, a 10-episode crime series set in Staten Island, Brooklyn, and Jersey (announced on Wiseguys)
  • Sells acting classes and life coaching through his IG bio

What the numbers say

  1. His story beats reposts. The prison-to-actor clip got about 10× the views of anything else about him. Reposted classic mob scenes (Pesci, Capone, Denzel) all stall around 100–150 likes, because they're someone else's moment.

  2. Gaetano is bigger than Chris right now. Gravesend content pulls 7–20× his own posts. Every post should send Gravesend fans to Chris, then to the film.

  3. Real life beats acting-for-camera. His best post is him and his wife jamming. It got the most comments because it shows who he really is.

  4. Other people's channels travel further. Podcasts, co-stars, and fans got him more reach than his own feed. Collab posts (one post that shows on both accounts) and clips cut from podcasts are the cheapest growth he has.

  5. The film has had almost no exposure. The trailer preview and festival posts are among his weakest. They have no hook, no story, and no place to buy.

05 · Communities and going global

Build the block, then the world

Communities to own

  • Gravesend fans: the biggest warm audience he has right now
  • Mob-movie and crime-story fans: the podcast circuit, Goodfellas and Casino meme pages
  • Italian-American families: clubs, feasts, Little Italy, Bensonhurst
  • Late starters and second chances: aspiring actors over 35, and people rebuilding their lives (only if Chris is comfortable with the prison story)
  • Indie filmmakers: the “17 Days” audience

Home base: an Instagram broadcast channel (“Chris Boy's Block”) for first looks, and an email list on the film's landing page. We own the list. No algorithm can take it away.

Going global

  • Distribution first: get a sales agent or aggregator so the film can be bought on digital stores. Without a buy button, viral reach doesn't turn into sales.
  • Subtitles: burned-in captions on every clip, with Italian, Spanish, and Portuguese versions of the top performers. Crime drama travels well in Italy and Latin America.
  • Italy angle: “Brooklyn Italian makes his own mob movie” pitched to Italian press and Italian-American outlets
  • International festivals: apply to more festivals abroad to get laurels and local press
  • Landing page: trailer, “get notified when it drops,” and press kit. LTGP can build and host it.
