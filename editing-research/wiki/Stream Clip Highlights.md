# Stream Clip Highlights

High-volume 15–60 s vertical clips of streamer moments (outbursts, IRL chaos, reactions), posted by networks of third-party clip accounts to TikTok, Shorts and Reels with the streamer's tag. Often many near-identical cuts of the same moment. A related format, **stream recaps**, stitches 5–8 moments from one stream into a sequential summary.

## How to do it
- **Single clip**: clip the moment with ~3 s of lead-in, reframe to 9:16 with the [[Facecam Stack Layout]], add [[Kinetic Captions]], keep the hook in the first second, use the streamer's tag. Join a campaign on Whop Content Rewards, Vyro, or the streamer's Discord.
- **Recap**: pick 5–8 peak moments in chronological order, add short title cards or captions between them, export vertical. Twitch announced a native **Recaps Feed** at Tokyo Game Show 2026 (no ship date).
- Voice-triggered clipping ("Twitch, clip that") is reported only in a social post **[unverified]**.

## Why it works
It is paid distribution dressed as fan content.
- N3on (Kick) paid **>$1.4M to 303 clippers in 5 weeks** at ~$40–50 per 100K views, partly reportedly funded by Kick (Tubefilter / Business Insider).
- CreatorDB (data vendor) counted **9,941 clips from 1,820 clipper accounts → 1.68B YouTube views + 260M TikTok plays in 30 days** for six streamers (IShowSpeed alone: 1.08B YouTube views).
- Payout rates: Whop ~$0.50–$1.50 per 1K views (~$40K/day payouts); MrBeast-backed Vyro ~$3 per 1K.
- Only ~10% of a community is live at any moment (Twitch CEO Dan Clancy), who partly credits Clips for rising viewership.

## Risks
Saturation and regulation: CreatorDB warns platforms are "deciding how much recycled content their feeds can hold" and flags gambling-content disclosure issues. Compare YouTube's Shorts originality update ([[Transformative Editing]]) and anti-aggregator moves on Snap and X.
