# SFX Layering

Using sound design as structure: whooshes or swipes on camera motion, subtle pops on text or icon reveals, amplified impacts (slam, coin drop) on reveals and punchlines, music changes at chapter turns, and dropping the music to silence just before a reveal.

## How to do it (AIR Media-Tech's numbers)
- Music under the voice at **−20 to −25 dB, 60–80 BPM** for calm narrative sections; **−8 to −12 dB, 100–120 BPM** for energetic builds.
- Land hard cuts on kicks or snares ([[Beat Sync]]).
- Don't put a pop on every subtitle; it feels over-processed.
- Tools: Epidemic Sound (AIR partner, a disclosed interest); [[Adobe Premiere Pro]] Essential Sound and Sept 2026 audio tools including Generate Sound Effects; Resolve Fairlight; [[Captions]] viral sound effects; [[Edits]] seasonal SFX (Jul 2026).

## Why it works
Sound effects are the cheapest pattern interrupt ([[Rehook]]). Music changes signal progress without words.
