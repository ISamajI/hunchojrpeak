# Green Screen Cutout

The subject (the creator, or a cut-out of a meme speaker) is placed over a photo, video, screenshot or news clip. It is used both as a visual punchline and as a commentary layout.

## How to do it
- **TikTok**: the Green Screen effect, or the "Cutout" feature (it gained a "Stroke" outline setting in Nov 2025, giving the sticker-like look).
- **[[CapCut]]**: Remove Background or Chroma Key over a background layer. Many trends spread as a CapCut template (e.g. "Why Would I Deceive You?").
- **YouTube Shorts "greenscreen explain"** (OpusClip): presenter on the left or right third, images, news clips or screenshots on the other side swapped every 4–6 s, clean voiceover and minimal music. Use the Shorts camera green screen (Social Media Today reported it generating AI backgrounds, ~2025, not opened) or CapCut.
- **X**: in-app green screen recording (July 2026 editor update), or chroma key in CapCut / Premiere for higher quality.

## Why it works
Quick context-setting with no location shoot. It lets you comment on visuals you don't own. Commentary counts as "adding value" under YouTube's Shorts originality update ([[Transformative Editing]]). On X, the research agent infers (X does not say this) that it counts as "original" more readily than a straight repost.

## Examples
TikTok: "Why Would I Deceive You?" (Nov 2026); "What year were you born" (Feb 2026, coworkers green-screened into Jurassic Park).
