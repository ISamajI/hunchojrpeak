# Speed Ramp

Playback speed changes inside a single clip: fast through the less important movement, a sharp slowdown at the action peak, then back up to speed, usually timed to the beat. "Velocity edit" is the name used in fan-edit and CapCut communities. "Time ramping" is the long-form YouTube name for slow-mo that snaps back to full speed.

## How to do it
- **CapCut Mobile** (per CapCutGuide, Jun 2026, updated Oct 2026):
  1. Select the clip, then **Edit > Speed > Curve > Custom**.
  2. Raise a curve point to speed up and lower it to slow down. Wider spacing between points gives a gentler change.
  3. Put the slow point on the action. The acceleration can start on a beat, with the slow point landing a few frames later on the movement.
  4. Automatic draft: **Edit > Style > Velocity** (Auto Velocity). It needs a sound so CapCut can detect beats. Treat the result as a starting draft.
  5. If the slow section is choppy, turn on Smooth slow-mo / optical flow or make the slowdown less extreme. Export a short test.
  - A Reddit snippet (Apr 2026, not verified) recommends an S-curve in the curve editor over the basic speed slider.
- **[[Premiere Mobile]]**: Speed Curves (added June 2026).
- **[[Edits]]**: speed curves were listed as upcoming on 2026-04-22. Shipping not confirmed in the raw notes.
- **[[DaVinci Resolve]]**: Speed Warp (faster in Resolve 21).
- **Long-form "time ramp"** (AIR Media-Tech): slow-mo into a snap back to full speed, used as a [[Rehook]] / pattern interrupt.
- For clean slow sections, shoot at 60fps or higher (see [[Cinematic Slow Motion]]).

## Why it works
The slow point puts the viewer's eye on the key moment, while the fast parts keep the energy up. It looks "edited" without any extra footage.
