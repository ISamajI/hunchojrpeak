# Variable Pacing

Committing to one deliberate rhythm pattern per long-form video (tight openings, calmer middles, bursts timed to story beats) instead of evenly fast cutting. It includes the post-"Beastification" slowdown toward story-first pacing.

## The five patterns (AIR Media-Tech)
- **Progressive Rhythm**: a visual change every 10–20 s for minutes 0–3, widening to 25–40 s, then calm stretches with short bursts after minute 8 (Veritasium, Ali Abdaal).
- **Contrast**: calm talking-head pacing (15–25 s per cut) broken every 2–3 min by 5–10 quick cuts (Ryan Trahan, Drew Gooden).
- **Narrative Loop**: return to the premise every 2–3 min with a title card or verbal callback (MrBeast, Yes Theory).
- **Hybrid Tempo**: fast micro-cuts during explanation, slow holds of up to 40 s on visuals (Better Ideas, Think Media).
- **Anchor**: cuts triggered by emotional beats rather than a timer, guided by ambient sound (Nathaniel Drew, Johnny Harris).

## How to do it
Pick a pattern at the script stage. Put timeline markers at each planned burst or rehook (Premiere: M; Resolve: M). Use smash cuts into bursts and strategic silence or pause cards out of them. Keep [[Jump Cut]]s for dead air but hold A-roll longer when the message carries itself; save heavy transitions for real turning points.
- **Audience calibration (AIR)**: ages 13–24, a visual change every 15–25 s; 25+, every 20–40 s and only on a topic or tone shift, with simple fades or light zooms.

## Why it works
Viewers learn a video's rhythm within the first minute. An intentional rhythm feels comfortable; flat or chaotic rhythm makes people leave. Contrast makes fast sections feel faster. MrBeast said (via X, 2024-03-03) views "skyrocketed" after he slowed down; Tubefilter counted 38 cuts/min (Mar 2023) vs 23 cuts/min (Mar 2024).
